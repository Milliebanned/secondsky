01

Try it with
test money

You need a browser wallet, such as MetaMask or Rabby. The quickest real result is a replay: pick a day that already happened and watch the contract settle it from two independent records.

  1. 01

    Get free Sepolia ETH

    Every transaction needs a little ETH for gas, even getting test USDC. Ask a faucet for some, for example Google's Sepolia faucet.

    You will see: 0.05 Sepolia ETH or so in your wallet. That is plenty.
    1 minfree
  2. 02

    Connect and get test USDC

    Open Cover a day, press Connect wallet, then Get 50 test USDC and approve it in your wallet.

    You will see: Connected · Sepolia, your address, and 50.00 test USDC.
    1 minone transaction
  3. 03

    Replay a past day

    Choose Replay a past day. Type a town and pick it from the list. Pick a day at least 5 days ago (NASA publishes late). Set a rain limit, for example 5 mm. Press Get a price, then Approve and buy.

    A replay costs the payout plus 2 USDC, so it can never make money. It exists so you can watch a full settlement today. For a real future day, use A future day instead.
    1 mintwo transactions
  4. 04

    Settle it

    Press Settle now. The contract itself buys two answers from Telegraph: one from an Open-Meteo miner and one from our NASA POWER miner.

    You will see: Settling, with two Telegraph job numbers. Answers usually arrive in about 8 minutes.
    ~8 minwaiting on Telegraph
  5. 05

    Finish and see the result

    When both bars show a number, press Finish. The keeper checks Telegraph's own records that the named miners really answered, then the contract pays by the rule.

    You will see one of: Paid 100% (both reached your limit), Paid 50% (they split), or Not paid (neither did). Each step links to its transaction.
    1 mindone

02

Check it without
trusting us

Every number on this site comes from a contract or from Telegraph's own records. These checks need only Node.js, and the first one needs no install at all.

Check 1 · no installRead a job straight from Telegraph

Every answer the contract paid on has a public record at Telegraph. It names the miner that answered and holds the raw answer. Open one in your browser: job 12, the NASA answer behind the Lagos 50% payout.

# or from a terminal
curl -A check https://devnode.telegraphprotocol.com/engine/v1/job/12/result
"miner_slug":"secondsky-nasa-rainfall",
"precipitation_mm":2.65, ...

Check 2 · read-only scriptVerify any policy end to end

This reads the policy from the contract, the two answers Telegraph delivered on-chain, and Telegraph's record of who answered each job. Then it applies the payout rule itself and says whether the contract did the same. No keys, no transactions.

git clone https://github.com/Milliebanned/secondsky
cd secondsky/scripts && npm install
node verify-policy.mjs 1
policy #1  6.4541,3.3947  2026-11-10
  source A: job 11 ... 17.6 mm
    answered by oathcast-forecast-verify (the named miner)
  source B: job 12 ... 2.65 mm
    answered by secondsky-nasa-rainfall (the named miner)
  the rule says: PaidHalf (10 USDC)
  the contract did: PaidHalf  ->  MATCHES

Check 3 · local chainRun the contract's tests

50 checks on a local chain: the payout rule, retries, the wrong-miner path, the finalize window, what each role can and cannot do, refunds, accounting, and the gas the Telegraph callback uses.

# terminal 1
cd secondsky/scripts
npx hardhat node --port 8545 --config hardhat.config.cjs
# terminal 2
node test-secondsky-v2.mjs
  callback gas estimate: 49708
  ✓ callback under 200k gas

50 passed, 0 failed

Check 4 · public dataRepeat the disagreement study

It pulls a year of daily rainfall for 10 cities from both public sources, with no keys, and counts how often they split. This is why the contract pays 50% on a split.

python3 scripts/agreement_study.py
limit  5 mm: split 569 (55% of days where at least one says wet)
limit 10 mm: split 375 (64%)
limit 20 mm: split 161 (73%)

Check 5 · our sourceAsk our NASA miner directly

Source B is our own Telegraph miner. You can call the same endpoint Telegraph calls. For a day that is not over yet, it refuses instead of guessing.

curl "https://secondsky-nasa.vercel.app/api/verify?location=Nairobi&date=2026-11-05"
NASA POWER records 0.61 mm of precipitation, a high of
29.09°C and a low of 16.05°C for Nairobi, Kenya on 2026-11-05

Check 6 · the failure we caughtSee Telegraph reroute a job

Job 14 was bought for our NASA miner, on a day NASA had not published yet. Telegraph's record shows a different miner answered it. The keeper caught it and sent the policy back. Nobody was paid on one source counted twice.

node verify-policy.mjs 2   # London, bought again after the catch
curl -A check https://devnode.telegraphprotocol.com/engine/v1/job/14/result
"miner_slug":"oathcast-forecast-verify"   # not the named NASA miner

The full list of every transaction and job is in RECEIPTS.md. The contracts' source is verified on Sourcify: SecondSky v2 and ForecastLog.

03

Try to break it

These should all fail safely. If one does not, that is a real bug, and we want to hear about it.

  • Settle a future day before it is over.The contract refuses with too early. Our NASA miner also refuses a day that has not ended, with window_not_finished.
  • Buy with a price older than 15 minutes.Refused with quote expired. Each signed price can be used once: quote used.
  • Change the price, payout or place in the transaction.The signature no longer matches: bad quote signature.
  • Finalize before both answers arrive.Refused with waiting for answers.
  • Finalize from your own wallet straight after the answers land.Anyone can finalize, but only one day after the last answer: operator check window. That day lets the keeper check that the named miners really answered.
  • Ask for a payout above the cap.Refused with payout above cap (50 test USDC while on testnet).
  • Leave a policy unsettled.After 7 days the buyer can take the premium back. Before that: grace period not over.
  • Pick a place where one source has no data.A failed answer sends the policy back to be bought again; the contract never pays on a failed answer. If Telegraph quietly gives the job to another miner instead, the keeper sees it in Telegraph's record and sends it back, within the one-day window above.
