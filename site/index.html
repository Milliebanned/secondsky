SecondSky — rain cover that asks two skies

Cover for one day that has to stay dry: a harvest, a spray run, a concrete pour. When the day is over, the contract buys that day's recorded rainfall from Telegraph twice, from two independent sources. It pays only when both agree it rained, and refuses to decide alone when they don't.

Every policy

Each bar is what one source said fell that day. The black line is the policy's rain limit. Every step links to its transaction.

What this is. A testnet run with test USDC. The policies so far are bought by our own test wallet, on days that have already passed, to exercise settlement against real recorded weather. They are not customers. Real cover would be sold before the day.

Why two sources

Most weather apps on Telegraph (and most parametric-insurance demos) read one upstream, usually Open-Meteo. Asking two miners that both read Open-Meteo is one opinion counted twice. NASA POWER is a separate pipeline (satellite precipitation and MERRA-2 reanalysis). The two often disagree on a given day, so a contract that pays on one of them pays on luck. SecondSky treats disagreement as a result, not an error.

Known limit, stated plainly: if a named miner does not answer, Telegraph can route the job to another miner, and which miner answered is not readable on-chain. The contract owner can therefore mark a policy Disputed after checking the job receipts. That can only block a payout, never force one.
