Three kinds of work
that cannot get wet

Some jobs happen on one booked day. If it rains, the machines stand still, the crew is still paid and the day is gone. Forecasts help you plan. They do not pay you when they are wrong.

01

Harvest

Grain cut wet has to be dried or sold for less. A rained-off day idles hired machines and crews.

03

Two skies decide.
Nobody else does.

One weather record can be wrong for your patch of ground. So the contract asks two that see the sky differently, and pays by a rule written before the day.

  1. i

    Pick the day

    Place, day, a rain limit and a payout. The price comes from ten years of both records at that exact spot, for the same two weeks of the year.

  2. ii

    The day ends

    The contract itself buys two answers from Telegraph through on-chain jobs, from two named miners: Open-Meteo archive (miner 5130) and NASA POWER satellite (miner 6151).

  3. iii

    Answers land

    About eight minutes later, both answers arrive on-chain. The keeper checks Telegraph's own records that the named miner really answered each job.

  4. iv

    The rule pays

    No claim form. No adjuster. The rule below, and a transaction anyone can check.

100%Both records reach the limit
50%They split
0Neither does
Real failure, caught

On 21 Nov we bought a day NASA had not published yet. Telegraph quietly gave that job to a different miner, which answered with the same number as the first source. The keeper compared Telegraph's job record with the miner the contract named, caught it, and sent the policy back. Nobody was paid on one source counted twice.

    06

    Was the forecast right?

    The morning before each covered day, the keeper buys Telegraph's forecast for that place through ForecastLog, and the network picks the miner. When the day settles, the forecast is scored against the two records the policy paid on. It "said rain" at a peak chance of 50% or more. The day "was wet" if the two records average 1 mm or more. The peak covers the whole window bought, which starts the day before, so it leans towards false alarms.

    07

    How often do two skies disagree?

    Open-Meteo against NASA POWER, daily rainfall, 10 cities, a full year (Nov 2025 to Oct 2026, 3,650 city-days). On most days both are dry. On days when at least one reaches the limit, they often split:

    55%at 5 mm
    of 1,036 rainy days
    64%at 10 mm
    of 587 rainy days
    73%at 20 mm
    of 221 rainy days

    A contract that trusts one record pays on that record's luck. The first version refused to pay on a split and left it to a person, which on these numbers means most rainy days end in review. Version 2 pays half, automatically, and prices that in. Method and code: agreement_study.py.

    08

    Who can do what

    Anyone
    can settle a due policy, and finalize it a day after both answers arrive.
    Operator
    a hot key on the quote server. Signs prices up to a 50 USDC payout cap and finalizes at once after checking Telegraph's job records. If the wrong miner answered, it can only send the policy back to be bought again. It cannot pick an outcome, move money or change settings.
    Owner
    a cold key that never touches a server. Controls the pool and the sources, and can withdraw only money not promised to open policies.
    Buyer
    if a policy is never settled, takes the premium back after 7 days.
